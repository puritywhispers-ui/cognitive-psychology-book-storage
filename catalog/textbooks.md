# Core textbooks

These are the survey books most often used in undergraduate and early graduate courses. Editions change; prefer the newest edition you can legally obtain, but older editions remain usable for history and core constructs.

## Primary course texts

### Eysenck, M. W., & Keane, M. T. — *Cognitive Psychology: A Student's Handbook*
- **Why it matters:** Widely treated as the standard comprehensive UK/European handbook. Strong on experimental tradition, memory, attention, language, and thinking.
- **Best for:** Semester survey; exam prep; finding the canonical experiment for a topic.
- **Pair with:** Primary papers listed in each chapter's references.
- **Note file:** `notes/eysenck-keane-students-handbook.md` (create from template)

### Goldstein, E. B. — *Cognitive Psychology: Connecting Mind, Research, and Everyday Experience*
- **Why it matters:** Very readable US intro text. Good figures, applied examples, and a gentle ramp into methods.
- **Best for:** First course; self-study if you are coming from outside psychology.

### Sternberg, R. J., & Sternberg, K. — *Cognitive Psychology*
- **Why it matters:** Broad coverage including intelligence, creativity, and problem solving, with Sternberg's own theoretical fingerprint.
- **Best for:** Readers who want cognition connected to individual differences.

### Anderson, J. R. — *Cognitive Psychology and Its Implications*
- **Why it matters:** Tight, theory-driven text from the ACT-R tradition. Excellent on learning, memory, and skill.
- **Best for:** Computationally minded readers; cognitive modeling.

### Groome, D. (and coauthors) — *An Introduction to Cognitive Psychology: Processes and Disorders*
- **Why it matters:** Links normal cognition to neuropsychology and clinical cases.
- **Best for:** Readers who want deficits (amnesia, agnosia, aphasia) as a window on function.

### Gazzaniga, M. S., Ivry, R. B., & Mangun, G. R. — *Cognitive Neuroscience: The Biology of the Mind*
- **Why it matters:** The standard bridge from cognitive psychology to systems neuroscience.
- **Best for:** When you need brain methods (fMRI, lesions, EEG) alongside cognitive constructs.

| *Handbook of Applied Cognition* | Durso (ed.) | Applied / human-factors turn |

## How to choose one textbook

- Want narrative + everyday examples → Goldstein.
- Want dense, exam-ready coverage → Eysenck & Keane.
- Want architecture and learning theory → Anderson.
- Want brain evidence from day one → Gazzaniga et al.
- Want clinical lesions as proof → Groome.
