# Language

Cognitive psychology of language asks how people acquire, parse, produce, and use linguistic structure in real time — not only what a grammar looks like on paper.

## Layers

- Phonology / speech perception
- Word recognition and the mental lexicon
- Syntax and parsing (garden paths, incrementality)
- Semantics and conceptual combination
- Pragmatics and common ground
- Production (lemma, word-form, articulation; speech errors as evidence)
- Reading and writing as visual-language skills
- Bilingual control

## Foundational tensions

- **Nativist vs. usage-based / constructivist** accounts of acquisition (Chomsky vs. Tomasello and statistical-learning programs).
- **Modularity** of the language faculty vs. domain-general prediction and control.
- **Mental lexicon** as dictionary vs. as a dynamic, distributed system.
- **Embodied / grounded** meaning vs. amodal symbols.

## Books

- Pinker — *The Language Instinct*; *Words and Rules*
- Harley — *The Psychology of Language*
- Traxler or Gaskell handbooks for graduate surveys
- Chomsky — primary linguistic theory (read with a psycholinguistics companion)
- Tomasello — usage-based acquisition
- Carroll or Gleason — psycholinguistics textbooks

## Classic empirical moves

- Speech errors and tip-of-the-tongue as production architecture evidence
- Cross-modal priming and lexical ambiguity resolution (Swinney)
- Garden-path sentences and eye-tracking in reading
- Categorical perception of phonemes
- N400 / P600 as electrophysiological signatures (bridge to neuroscience)

## Caution

Do not treat popular nativism-vs-empiricism fights as settled. Store both the linguistic arguments and the developmental / corpus / neural data that later constrained them.
