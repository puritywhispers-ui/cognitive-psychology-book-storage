# Attention

Attention is selection plus control: which information gets processed, how deeply, and at what cost to other processing.

## Core questions

- Is the filter early (Broadbent) or late (Deutsch & Deutsch), or is it more like attenuation (Treisman)?
- What is selected — locations, objects, features, or learned categories?
- How do alerting, orienting, and executive networks divide the labor (Posner)?
- When does selection fail (Stroop, inattentional blindness, change blindness, attentional blink)?

## Models to keep straight

| Family | Idea | Anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bottleneck / filter | Limited channel; unattended stream is blocked or damped | Broadbent 1958; Treisman 1960 |
| Feature integration | Features register in parallel; binding needs focal attention | Treisman & Gelade 1980 |
| Spotlight / zoom | Spatial selection with a movable, resizable window | Posner cueing |
| Load theory | Perceptual load determines whether distractors leak in | Lavie |
| Networks | Alerting, orienting, executive control as separable systems | Posner & Petersen |

## Experiments that still teach the concepts

- Dichotic listening and shadowing (Cherry)
- Posner endogenous vs. exogenous cueing
- Visual search: feature vs. conjunction; pop-out vs. serial feel
- Stroop interference
- Attentional blink (RSVP)
- Inattentional blindness (Gorilla / Simons & Chabris) as a public demo, with lab controls

## Note hooks

When you write a note, separate:

1. **Capacity claims** (how much) from **selection claims** (what gets in).
2. **Behavioral signatures** from **neural networks**.
3. **Laboratory dual-task costs** from **everyday multitasking folklore**.
