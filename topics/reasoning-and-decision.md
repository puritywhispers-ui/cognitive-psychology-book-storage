# Reasoning, judgment, and decision making

This area sits on the border of cognitive psychology, social psychology, and economics. Keep the *task* (logic problem, probability judgment, choice under risk) separate from the *theory class* (heuristics-and-biases, dual process, ecological rationality, resource-rational analysis).

## Theory classes

| Class | Claim | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heuristics and biases | Minds use shortcuts that produce systematic error relative to norms | Norms themselves can be disputed |
| Dual process | Fast intuitive vs. slow deliberative systems | "System 1/2" is a metaphor; mechanisms differ by model |
| Fast-and-frugal / ecological rationality | Heuristics can be accurate in the right environment | Needs specified ecology |
| Bayesian / resource-rational | Apparent biases can be rational under costs and priors | Easy to become unfalsifiable |
| Prospect theory | Value is reference-dependent; losses loom larger | Descriptive of choice, not a process model |

## Must-read sources

- Tversky & Kahneman (1974) heuristics-and-biases
- Kahneman & Tversky (1979) prospect theory
- Kahneman (2011) *Thinking, Fast and Slow*
- Gigerenzer work on ecological rationality (counterweight)
- Evans, Stanovich, Kahneman dual-process variants — do not collapse them
- Baron — *Thinking and Deciding* (textbook)
- Hastie & Dawes — *Rational Choice in an Uncertain World*

## Standard effects to file correctly

- Availability, representativeness, anchoring
- Base-rate neglect and conjunction fallacy
- Framing and loss aversion
- Overconfidence (several kinds; do not lump)
- Confirmation bias / myside bias
- Wason selection task and content effects
- Dual-process accounts of belief bias in syllogisms

## How to take notes here

Always write:

1. The **normative standard** being used (logic, probability, expected utility).
2. The **descriptive finding**.
3. At least one **alternative interpretation** (task misunderstanding, different goal, ecological match).
