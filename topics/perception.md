# Perception

Perception is organized inference from sensory data, constrained by the structure of the world and the goals of action.

## Two deep traditions

1. **Constructive / computational**  
   Helmholtz → information processing → Marr. The system recovers a description of the world from inherently ambiguous images by using constraints, priors, and multiple levels of representation.

2. **Ecological / direct**  
   Gibson. The optic array already specifies affordances; perception is pickup of invariants, not inference over impoverished cues.

Most modern work is hybrid: Bayesian and predictive-processing accounts are constructive; embodied and active-vision work keeps Gibson's emphasis on movement and information in the ambient array.

## Marr's three levels (use this as a thinking tool everywhere)

1. **Computational** — what is being computed and why?
2. **Algorithmic / representational** — what representations and procedures?
3. **Implementational** — how do neurons or silicon do it?

Confusing these levels produces most bad arguments in the field.

## Landmark topics

- Iconic memory and brief visual display (Sperling)
- Pattern recognition: template, feature, prototype, structural description
- Object recognition: Biederman geons vs. view-based models
- Two visual streams: ventral "what" / dorsal "how" (Goodale & Milner)
- Face perception and expertise debates
- Perceptual organization (Gestalt) as still-useful descriptive laws
- Multisensory integration and prior weighting
