# Beginner path (8–12 weeks of serious self-study)

Assume 5–7 hours a week. Goal: own the vocabulary and the classic effects well enough to read a review paper without drowning.

## Week 1–2 — What the field is

- Read a short history chapter (Gardner *The Mind's New Science* excerpts, or the opening of Neisser 1967 + a modern textbook preface).
- Skim Miller (1956) using the example note in `notes/examples/`.
- Write one page: behaviorism vs. information-processing vs. later embodied / neural turns.

## Week 3–4 — Attention and perception

- Textbook chapters on attention and perception (Goldstein or Eysenck & Keane).
- Cherry cocktail party; Posner cueing; Treisman FIT; Sperling iconic memory.
- Optional demo: inattentional blindness videos, then read a methods-careful paper so you do not overgeneralize the demo.

## Week 5–7 — Memory

- Modal model (Atkinson & Shiffrin) as history.
- Baddeley working memory.
- Levels of processing; encoding specificity; episodic vs. semantic (Tulving).
- Reconstructive memory (Bartlett; Loftus).
- Practice: build a 30-card Anki deck of *constructs*, not trivia.

## Week 8–9 — Language and thinking

- Mental lexicon and parsing at textbook level.
- Judgment: Tversky & Kahneman 1974 plus Kahneman 2011 selected chapters (Systems language, then go back to the paper).

## Week 10 — Integration

- Pick one applied domain (eyewitness memory, education, HCI, clinical assessment).
- Write a 800–1200 word briefing that cites at least one textbook chapter and two primary papers.

## What not to do first

- Do not start with Marr *Vision* or Newell & Simon unless you already like formal models.
- Do not treat *Thinking, Fast and Slow* as a methods course.
- Do not download illegal textbooks "just for study." Use library, used print, or publisher rental.
